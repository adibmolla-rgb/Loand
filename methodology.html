What LoanD is, and isn't

LoanD helps you see whether your figures fall within mainstream UK lenders' published mortgage and personal loan limits, and compare what lenders publish side by side. It is not a lender, broker or adviser.

  • It never says you "will be approved". Lenders also check your credit history, spending and the property using models they don't publish.
  • It doesn't rank or score lenders. You choose how to sort.
  • Nothing on it is financial advice or an offer of credit. Check with the lender or an FCA-authorised adviser before you apply.
  • Everything is worked out in your browser. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.

How the estimates work

  • Within or outside published limits. Your loan-to-value, income multiple, loan size, term, age, repayment type and (if self-employed) years trading are compared with each lender's published numeric rules. Only CONFIRMED rules decide the result. Where several limits apply, the strictest wins, except income multiples: lenders publish those as tables of income and LTV bands, so the highest multiple in your band applies, and a debt-to-income rule can then lower it.
  • Special routes (for example family-assisted mortgages) are listed separately and never used to judge your figures.
  • Indicative maximum borrowing is the lowest of a lender's published caps (income multiple, LTV and maximum loan) for your figures. Lenders' own affordability models aren't public, so what a lender would actually lend can be lower.
  • Repayments use the standard repayment or interest-only formulas at the rate you enter, held for the whole term. Fees, insurance and legal costs are not included.
  • Purchase tax uses the official bands in the research for England and Northern Ireland (SDLT), Scotland (LBTT) and Wales (LTT), including first-time buyer relief where it exists and the higher rates for additional homes.
  • Estimated cost over a deal is the interest paid during the initial period plus the product fee. It assumes the fee is paid up front and the rate is fixed for the period.
  • Personal loan payments use the lender's published APR as if it were the rate you'd be offered. Your offer may be higher.
  • Historical data (the Market page) comes from Bank of England series and is always labelled as history, never as a current rate.
